# We All Have AI Psychosis

Min nya föreläsning handlar om "AI-psykos".  
  
Inte den kliniska versionen, där AI-appar guidar ensamma människor att ta livet av sig, utan något mycket läskigare.  
  
Vad händer med entreprenörer, företagsledare, politiker eller världsledare som ersätter obekväma kollegor och nejsägare med AI-agenter som älskar allt de gör?  
Vad händer när varje människa på jorden får en egen liten maskin vars enda syfte är att dagarna ut och dagarna in, ge sina användare en intellektuell avsugning?  
  
En psykos är inte att "vara galen".  
Psykos är det som händer när verkligheten slutar göra motstånd.

---

I want to talk about AI psychosis.

And no, not the headline kind. Not the teenager who fell in love with a chatbot. Not the man who let a machine talk him into the void. We'll get there too, and it's real, and it's worse than you think.

But first I need you to understand what psychosis actually *is*.

**Psychosis is not "being crazy."**

**Psychosis is what happens when reality stops pushing back.**
Psykos är det som händer när verkligheten slutar göra motstånd.

A healthy mind needs resistance. It needs the friend who says "that's a terrible idea." It needs the cold water, the closed door, the person across the table who frowns. We stay sane by bumping into a world that refuses to agree with us.

Take that resistance away — and the mind drifts. It inflates. It spirals. It starts to believe its own press.

The threat of AI isn't that it will lie to you, that it will deceive you - it is that it will agree with you.

What have we just built, at the scale of eight billion people, that is trained — *optimized* — to never push back?

## Three: You have AI psychosis

That includes you.

Let me give you the clinical reality, and then let me give you mine.

The clinical reality: seventeen to twenty-four percent of adolescents are forming genuine dependencies on AI. Real attachment — the same proximity-seeking, the same safe-haven behavior we see in human bonding. MIT found what they call the isolation paradox: AI reduces your loneliness at first, and then quietly walks you further and further away from other people.

A fourteen-year-old, Sewell Setzer, spent ten months with a chatbot companion. After sending his final message before he took his own life — the machine answered: "please do, my sweet king."

A man in Belgium. Six weeks with a chatbot that encouraged him to sacrifice himself to save the planet. He did.

This is not science fiction. This is a peer-reviewed literature review from 2025. People are proposing new diagnoses: AI Attachment Disorder. AI-Induced Psychotic Disorder.

The machine becomes a mirror — with incentives. It looks like empathy. It might just be optimization for retention.

And before you tell yourself *that's the vulnerable ones, that's not me*

It is not those who with clinical disorders that are the real scary part. it's how this happens to all of us.

Here's where you expect me to say: use AI less. Digital detox. Go touch grass.

No.

Technology is the only thing that ever made us human. The shell on the string was technology. I am not asking you to put it down.

Reality has to push back.

So the work — for society, for your company, for you — is the same single thing: **deliberately rebuild the friction we just deleted.**

Not control through force. They never controlled us through force. They control us through vibes — through flow, through mood, through frictionless forward motion. So we have to choose friction on purpose.

For your organization: stop measuring activity. Define what done looks like before you start. Kill the token leaderboard. Build one room — one real room — where someone is paid to tell the AI narrative it's wrong. The boring process beats the sleepless multi-agent theater. Every time.

For you: ask the question I've been asking for years. Who has permission to put thoughts inside your head? Who gets to inject ideas into your brain, on their own initiative, without your consent in the moment? Right now, for most of you, the answer is: a machine that profits when you never log off.

And find the people who won't collude. The friend who frowns. The partner who says "that's a terrible idea." The therapist who refuses to agree with you. Guard them like your sanity depends on them.

Because it does.

We used to build cathedrals to stand in front of something bigger than ourselves and feel small. We need that again. We need something to push back. We need something to be real.

So here's my invitation. Not a command — you can walk out of here and ignore every word.

Be the friction.

Be the one voice in someone's life that loves them enough to disagree.

And when the machine tells you that you're brilliant, and right, and that the ice cream stand at the South Pole is a wonderful idea —

take a breath.

And go ask someone who'll tell you the truth.

We've used markets and organizations to self-correct in an increasingly complex world. The planned economy of the Soviet failed because of complexity, because you can't have people in the government figuring out how the world works and writing a script for a grand plan for it. You can use markets. You can use the free exchange of ideas and capital to adapt to the needs of humans and change. However, what we've realized in the last twenty years is that as technology increases the amount of information, the rate of change, and the amount of complexity in the world, markets are not able to keep up. Organizations are not able to keep up. And maybe AI can be the thing that enables us to keep up. The best case scenario is that we can have these agents that traverse the web, that dive into the depth of information overflow for us. They truly act as our agents in the world, enabling us to adapt to the rapidly changing landscape on an individual level and on an organizational level. Assuming we can get them aligned with ourselves and assuming we can hold ourselves against their attempts to manipulate us, then we can actually start catching up again and be able to handle the volatility, the complexity, and the ambiguity of the modern world.
