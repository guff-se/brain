A sweeping bearish piece about the economics under the AI boom. Zitron’s claim is not merely that valuations are overheated, but that the whole stack — model labs, hyperscalers, GPU vendors, and data-center financiers — now depends on sustained hypergrowth in AI demand that may be impossible to deliver.

## Key claims

- The infrastructure buildout has outrun plausible demand. Citing 190GW of planned data-center capacity and Jensen Huang’s estimate of $80B–$100B per gigawatt, Zitron argues the implied capex is roughly $9.5T–$15T.
- The revenue hurdle is extreme. He argues AI / compute would need to generate on the order of $2T+ in annual revenue by 2030 — with even a half-built version of the planned capacity still requiring enormous yearly revenue to stay solvent.
- Demand is dangerously concentrated. OpenAI and Anthropic are framed as the main real buyers of frontier compute, meaning the market story depends on two highly unprofitable companies continuing to scale at near-impossible rates.
- NVIDIA’s boom is also concentration risk. Zitron notes that 54% of NVIDIA revenue comes from three customers, making the supply chain look broad in public while remaining narrow underneath.
- The financing mix is getting shakier. The piece treats giant debt needs, hyperscaler equity raises, and escalating model-lab commitments as signs that the sector increasingly relies on capital markets staying permanently open.
- The rest of the market is too small. Even aggressive enterprise spending on AI services is presented as insufficient relative to the scale of compute being financed and promised.

## Why it matters here

Useful for the money section of [[We All Have AI Psychosis]] and for the broader claim that collective AI psychosis is not just cultural or cognitive but financial. The article sharpens a core frame: an entire economy can become too invested in an inevitability story to let reality push back on it.
