A combative essay about the economics and culture around the current US frontier-model market. The author argues that companies and developers are overpaying for prestige, geography, and parasocial attachment while model quality is flattening enough that Chinese alternatives — especially Qwen 3.7 Max — now break the old price-to-capability logic.

## Key claims

- The current AI market is shaped as much by taste, branding, and parasocial attachment as by raw capability; the author frames this as an “OnlyFans economy” where users and investors keep simping for premium model providers.
- US frontier labs, especially Anthropic, are accused of charging a premium that no longer matches the actual quality gap, particularly for sustained engineering work rather than one-shot demos.
- Cheaper Chinese models are closing or crossing the practical usefulness threshold. Qwen 3.7 Max is presented as the strongest example: a model good enough for long-running engineering work at a much lower price point.
- Enterprise spend is outrunning evidence of value. The piece points to stories like companies burning through huge token budgets, layoffs justified by AI, and meter-based billing that rewards activity before outcomes are proven.
- Benchmarks matter less than lived work. The author trusts longitudinal, hands-on usage across real projects over short benchmark tests or prestige narratives.
- The broader financial story is a bubble dynamic: inflated valuations, investor theater, and ordinary downstream people eventually eating the losses if the economics fail to justify themselves.
