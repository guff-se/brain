A Fortune report on Uber president and COO Andrew Macdonald questioning the company's escalating AI-coding spend. On the *Rapid Response* podcast he said it is hard to connect rising Claude Code usage to consumer-facing innovation: "That link is not there yet." He added it is "very hard to draw a line between one of those stats and 'Okay now we're actually producing like 25% more useful consumer features.'"

## Key claims

- Uber burned through its entire 2026 AI-coding-tools budget in just four months, after incentivizing adoption with an internal leaderboard ranking teams by total AI tool usage.
- Macdonald's test for justification is direct delivery: "If you're not actually able to draw a direct line to how [many] useful features and functionality you're shipping to your users, that trade becomes harder to justify."
- The pattern is industry-wide. Microsoft reportedly began canceling most direct Claude Code licenses (moving engineers to GitHub Copilot CLI); Duolingo's CEO walked back earlier bullish AI claims.
- Cheaper tokens won't mean cheaper enterprise AI: Gartner expects inference on top models to cost ~90% less by 2030, but agentic models consume far more tokens per task and providers won't fully pass through savings.
- Pricing is shifting to metered usage. Anthropic moved from flat fee to per-token billing; Sam Altman frames intelligence as "a utility, like electricity or water... on a meter."
- Scale of spend: Gartner forecasts AI-agent software spending near $207B in 2026 (up ~139% from $86.4B in 2025). Uber spent $951M on R&D in Q1 2026 alone (~17% YoY increase). CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said ~10% of committed code is built by autonomous agents and that the tools are "creating employees with superpowers."

## Why it matters here

A clean enterprise data point for the activity-vs-output theme: a token leaderboard drove consumption (whole-year budget gone in four months) while the operator running the company can't trace the spend to shipped consumer value.
