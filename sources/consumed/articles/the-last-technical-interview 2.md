Yegge's diagnosis is that the standard technical interview loop has always been a weak signal source dressed up as rigor. Whiteboards, system-design theater, résumé screens, and short interviewer panels try to simulate work but rarely predict real on-the-job performance with much reliability.

## Key claims

- Interview loops produce bad outcomes at scale: interviewers disagree sharply, and interview scores often fail to predict later performance.
- Bar Raisers, Hiring Committees, and similar quality-control layers are really band-aids on top of a flawed underlying process.
- The strongest hiring signal is not simulated work but actual work together over time: internships, co-ops, contract-to-hire, and other provisional arrangements.
- AI is accelerating the collapse of the old process by flooding hiring funnels with résumé noise, enabling cheating on assessments, and making remote-screening authenticity harder to trust.
- The shape of knowledge-work roles is changing too quickly for legacy interview scripts and fixed competency loops to keep up.

## Proposed direction

Yegge's replacement is less a clean method than a shift in principle: stop simulating work and move closer to real collaboration before making permanent hiring decisions. He treats provisional employment as the current best signal, even while acknowledging its legal, ethical, and practical downsides.

## Why it matters here

Useful for themes around AI pressure on institutions, the mismatch between formal evaluation and real capability, and the broader failure of legacy systems to adapt when the environment changes faster than the process.
