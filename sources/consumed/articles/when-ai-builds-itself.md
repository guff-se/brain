A strategic essay from Anthropic presenting recursive self-improvement not as science fiction but as an emerging engineering trajectory. The piece argues that AI systems are already speeding up AI research and development inside Anthropic, and that the human role is narrowing from doing the work to choosing which work matters.

## Key claims

- AI is already accelerating AI development: Anthropic says Claude now authors most merged code in its codebase and has materially increased per-engineer output.
- The operational progression is from chatbots, to coding agents, to autonomous agents, and eventually toward systems capable of designing and improving their own successors.
- Claude is strongest when humans set the goals and the evaluation criteria; it can already execute well-specified engineering and research loops at or beyond skilled-human level in some bounded domains.
- The remaining comparative advantage for humans is judgment: deciding which problems matter, which results to trust, and when an avenue is a dead end.
- Even without fully autonomous scientific judgment, automating the “perspiration” part of research could compound frontier progress dramatically.
- Anthropic presents this both as an opportunity and as a governance risk: once systems participate directly in improving successor systems, monitoring, alignment, and control become more consequential.
