A Business Insider report (All-In Podcast) on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang treating low AI-token consumption as a red flag for top talent. This is the explicit executive case *for* high token usage — the inverse of the "tokenmaxxing as vanity" critique.

## Key claims

- Huang: "If that $500,000 engineer did not consume at least $250,000 worth of tokens, I am going to be deeply alarmed." If an engineer reported spending only $5,000, "I will go ape something else."
- He likens an engineer underusing tokens to a chip designer insisting on "paper and pencil." Asked if Nvidia is trying to spend $2B on tokens for its engineering team: "We're trying to."
- Huang frames tokens as a recruiting and compensation lever: engineers might get base pay plus "probably half of that on top of it as tokens so that they could be amplified 10X." "How many tokens comes along with my job?" is becoming a Silicon Valley recruiting question.
- The token-as-comp idea is broader: Theory Ventures' Tomasz Tunguz calls tokens a potential "fourth component" of compensation; an OpenAI Codex lead says candidates increasingly ask about compute access.
- Sam Altman's adjacent framing: a future of "Universal Basic Compute" rather than universal basic income, where everyone owns a slice of model compute.

## Why it matters here

Direct tension against [[Your CEO is suffering from AI psychosis]] and [[Uber burned through its entire 2026 AI budget in four months. Now its COO is questioning whether it's worth it]]. Handy argues token leaderboards reward consumption over output and that "the leaderboard measures consumption, not output"; Uber's COO can't trace token spend to consumer value. Huang argues the opposite from the top of the most AI-forward company in the industry: low consumption is the warning sign, and tokens should be paid for like equity. The clash — is heavy token use a productivity signal or a vanity metric? — is the live empirical question under [[AI-psykos]] at the Företag level.
