McGrann's core claim is that current AI valuations only make sense if the industry captures the global labor market. In that frame, "assistant" rhetoric is marketing and the real product is labor replacement.

## Key claims

- AI firms and their investors need labor displacement at massive scale to justify current valuations.
- Individual firms are rewarded for automating even when system-wide demand collapses, creating a collective self-destruction dynamic akin to a prisoner's dilemma.
- This is not just an employment problem but a democratic one: when citizens are no longer needed as workers, taxpayers, or consumers, their political leverage shrinks.
- The likely result without redistribution or restraint is sharper wealth concentration, weaker democratic accountability, and stronger alignment between frontier AI firms and authoritarian power.
- UBI or retraining alone do not solve the deeper loss of status, meaning, and social cohesion that follows mass economic displacement.

## Named frames and references

McGrann connects the argument to Wharton's "The AI Layoff Trap," Acemoglu on excessive automation, Leontief's horse analogy, Piketty on capital concentration, Mazzucato on rent extraction, and Case/Deaton on deaths of despair.
